Building an analyst relations function from scratch

Uniphore's enterprise buyers wanted analyst validation before they signed, and the company wasn't showing up in the research they read. I set up an AR function that changed that.

Context

Uniphore sells conversational AI to large enterprises, mainly for contact centres and customer experience. I was the global PMM owner for U-Assist, the real-time agent assist product, and the platform PMM for JAPAC and EMEA.

For enterprise buyers, analyst research is part of due diligence. Before committing to a vendor, their evaluation teams check where it sits in reports from firms like Gartner and Forrester.

The problem

Uniphore had no active analyst relations function, and it wasn't being featured in many reports. That showed up in sales: enterprise customers needed analyst validation, and without it, deals took longer to close.

AR wasn't the only thing slowing the sales cycle, but it was a friction point we could remove.

My role

I owned

Setting up the AR process, choosing which analysts to engage, and running the ongoing relationships. I was also the person briefing the analysts.

I worked with

Internally: product owners; the CMO, CPO, and VP of Product Marketing; sales leaders; an AR project manager; and customer success, who helped line up customer references and use cases.

Externally: the analysts themselves, and our customers, who spoke with analysts as references.

Approach: key decisions

  1. Focus on the analysts our buyers actually read

    Rather than trying to brief every firm, I identified which analysts covered our market and influenced enterprise CX buyers, and prioritized those relationships.

    Why: Analyst time is limited, and so was ours. Mentions only help sales if they appear in the research buyers check during evaluations.

  2. Meet every priority analyst every month

    I streamlined how Uniphore engaged analysts, with a clear owner and a monthly briefing with each analyst on their own cadence.

    Why: Recognition comes from analysts knowing a company over time. One-off conversations don't lead to repeat mentions.

  3. Keep analysts updated between briefings

    I created a monthly analyst digest covering what was new in the product, new customers, strategic company updates, customer soundbites, new case studies, and how our product team had used analysts' input.

    Why: Analysts cover many vendors. A regular, proactive update kept Uniphore's momentum visible, and showing how we acted on their feedback made the relationship two-way.

  4. Turn analyst recognition into a sales tool

    Analyst mentions went into every collateral and sales playbook, and sales used them in pitch decks and RFP responses. I also pulled out the most useful findings, such as our strengths and how we compared with competitors, and fed them into sales enablement and competitive intelligence.

    Why: The goal wasn't recognition for its own sake. It was giving sales a credible, third-party answer to "why choose you over a competitor?"

What I built

Results

  • Repeat leader and challenger mentions in research from Gartner, IDC, Forrester, Everest Group, and Opus Research
  • Featured in the Gartner Magic Quadrant for CCaaS, 2023 and 2024 [more reports to add]
  • Analyst mentions built into every collateral, sales playbook, pitch deck, and RFP response, and into sales enablement and competitive intelligence, giving sales a strong case for why to choose Uniphore over a competitor
  • Enterprise buyers could now find the analyst validation they looked for, removing one source of friction from long sales cycles

What I'd do differently

I'd start the monthly analyst digest from day one. Sharing what we were doing proactively, rather than waiting for briefings, would have built analysts' awareness of Uniphore even faster.

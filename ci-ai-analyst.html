Context

It started as a group capstone at the AI Accelerator Bootcamp run by Garage Labs Tech with Shiv Nadar University and Atal Innovation Mission, built on a synthetic dataset. I then built the full product on my own. It is aimed at product managers, product marketers, founders, and GTM leaders at mid-size B2B SaaS and IT companies.

It draws on competitor websites, pricing pages, release notes, G2 and Capterra reviews, news and PR, and analyst mentions.

The problem

Today, these teams spend 5–8 hours a week scanning competitor websites, reviews, news, and analyst mentions, tab by tab. Around 70% of that time goes to collecting data and only 30% to analysis, so insights are shallow and already stale by the time they reach leadership. The same findings then get rebuilt into separate documents for leadership, product, and sales.

General-purpose LLMs don't solve it: even with careful prompting, they return incomplete or inaccurate answers. And the stakes are rising. AI has compressed feature development from months to weeks, so competitors can reprice, reposition, or ship faster than any manual process can track.

Approach: key decisions

  1. One analysis, five audiences

    Every run produces a full SWOT and strategy memo, and automatically turns it into four more outputs: a leadership summary (under 250 words), a product gap table with roadmap signals, a sales battlecard (150–250 words, ready to hand to an AE), and a founder view (under 150 words).

    Why: The biggest waste in CI isn't only research. It's rebuilding the same findings for every stakeholder. One run, with no reformatting, removes that.

  2. Two lanes of evidence, with honest confidence

    Live data is scraped on demand from competitor websites, reviews, news, and analyst mentions, and ranked first. A curated dataset of 176 documents fills in when live results are thin. Findings backed only by the fallback data are capped at MEDIUM confidence; findings corroborated by live data can be HIGH.

    Why: Users need to know how much to trust each finding. Tying confidence to where the evidence came from makes that visible.

  3. Use the LLM only where it adds value

    The pipeline is deterministic: open-source models handle entity extraction, theme classification, and sentiment. Claude Haiku routes the query and re-ranks results, and only the final synthesis uses Claude Sonnet, with GPT-4o as a fallback. I kept it a fixed-sequence (Level 1) agent rather than a multi-agent system.

    Why: This keeps each full analysis at an estimated ₹2.73–4.17, under a ₹5 ceiling, and makes results predictable. A more complex agent design wasn't justified by the use case.

  4. Build trust into every output

    Every claim is sourced with a document ID and date, and carries a confidence level. Claims older than 14 days are flagged as stale, and unverified claims are set apart from the main findings. The product refuses to guess about internal roadmaps, give investment advice, write fake reviews, or analyse competitors outside its sources, and it gives a balanced analysis even when asked to skip weaknesses.

    Why: Competitive intelligence is only useful if people trust it. When evidence is missing, it says "Insufficient evidence" instead of making something up.

Results

  • Two live versions: the full product I built, and the group version on synthetic data
  • One run produces five ready-to-use outputs, designed to replace 5–8 hours of weekly manual research
  • Estimated cost of ₹2.73–4.17 per full analysis, with a target of 15–30 seconds end to end
  • Tested against a known-answer evaluation set, including questions it should decline to answer

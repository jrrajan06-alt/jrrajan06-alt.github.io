Context

Through my fractional PMM practice, A Saasy Lassie, I work with early-stage startups in procurement tech, climate tech, and hospitality. One of them, Pentimenti, works in Strategic Response Management: software that helps companies respond to RFPs, proposals, and tenders.

The problem

Startups rarely have what analysts usually look for: years of market presence, large customer bases, and big marketing budgets. The largest firms may not cover a category until it is established.

So the question was where to start, and what a young company could show that would make analysts want to keep talking.

My role

I owned

Analyst relations for each startup: choosing which firms to approach, preparing briefings, and setting up the ongoing engagement structure.

Approach: key decisions

  1. Match the firm to the company's stage

    For smaller companies, I approach industry-specific firms and advisors such as Everest Group, Opus Research, and McKinsey. For scale-ups, I first identify the category, then approach IDC, Forrester, and Gartner.

    Why: Specialist firms are more open to young companies and know their niche well. The largest firms become worthwhile once a startup can clearly place itself in a category they cover.

  2. Look at where competitors are featured

    Part of choosing firms is checking which analysts already cover a startup's competitors.

    Why: If analysts are writing about your competitors, they are already researching your market and are likely to want your view too.

  3. Lead with what makes the company different

    Briefings focus on the startup's cutting-edge technology, the business problem it solves, and customer references. Bringing customers onto briefings helps most.

    Why: A young company can't compete on size, but it can show strong technology solving a real problem. Customers on the call make that proof credible.

  4. Set up a structure from day one

    Each startup gets regular briefings, a monthly digest, and broader quarterly updates.

    Why: This is the lesson I took from earlier roles: proactive, regular updates build analyst awareness much faster than one-off conversations.

What I built

Results

  • A structured analyst relations program in place for four startups: regular briefings, monthly digests, and quarterly updates
  • McKinsey invited further discussions in the Strategic Response Management (SRM) space
  • Startups enter analyst conversations with a clear story: their technology, the problem they solve, and customer proof
